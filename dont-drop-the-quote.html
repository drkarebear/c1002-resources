Don't Drop the Quote

Textual evidence works best when your reader understands where it comes from, why you chose it, and what you want them to notice about it.

What This Helps You Do

This lab helps you use quotations as part of your own reasoning instead of dropping them into a paragraph and moving on.

1. Give ContextHelp the reader understand the moment without retelling the whole story.
2. Integrate the EvidenceMake the quotation fit naturally into your sentence.
3. Analyze ItReturn to your own voice and explain what the evidence does.
Think bridge, not dump. Your sentence should carry the reader into the quotation and then back into your analysis.

EVIDENCE TO USE

You practiced using quotations as part of your own thinking.

Before you add a quotation, ask:

  • Does my reader need a little context?
  • Can I integrate the evidence into my own sentence?
  • Have I returned to my own voice afterward?
  • Am I explaining the important language instead of repeating it?

A quotation gives you evidence. Your analysis tells the reader why that evidence matters.
