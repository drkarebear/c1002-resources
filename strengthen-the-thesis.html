Strengthen the Thesis

A literary-analysis thesis should do more than name a topic or repeat an observation. It should make a specific, arguable interpretation that gives your essay something meaningful to develop.

What This Helps You Do

Think of thesis development as a ladder. Each step adds more interpretation.

TOPICNames the subject.
OBSERVATIONNotices a pattern or detail.
BASIC CLAIMMakes an interpretation, but may still be broad or obvious.
STRONGER THESISMakes a specific, arguable claim that explains why the pattern matters.
A thesis is the result of thinking, not a sentence formula. Phrases like “the author uses symbolism to show…” can be useful scaffolds, but they do not automatically create an interesting argument.

STARTING IDEA

You practiced strengthening a thesis.

Before you settle on a thesis, ask:

  • Does this do more than name my topic?
  • Does it explain what a pattern or detail may suggest?
  • Is the claim specific enough to develop with evidence?
  • Is it arguable rather than obvious?
  • Does it avoid claiming more than the text can support?

A strong thesis gives your essay direction because it tells the reader not only what you noticed, but what you think that pattern means.
