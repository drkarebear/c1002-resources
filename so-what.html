So What?

A useful analytical question opens the door. The next step is to consider a possible interpretation: an explanation of what the detail may suggest and why it matters.

Before You Practice: Make the Significance Clearer, Not Bigger

“So what?” does not mean you have to make a sweeping statement about all people, all society, or all of human nature. It means explaining why your interpretation matters inside the text.

OBSERVATIONWhat specific detail do you notice?
→
PATTERNWhat relationship develops across details?
→
INTERPRETATIONWhat might the pattern suggest?
→
SIGNIFICANCEWhy does that interpretation matter here?
Significance is not the same as a universal life lesson. A precise explanation of what the pattern changes, reveals, complicates, costs, or makes possible in this text is often a stronger “So What?” than a giant statement about humanity.

A useful “So What?” usually does three things:

  • stays connected to the interpretation you already built,
  • clarifies what the pattern reveals, complicates, costs, or changes, and
  • avoids becoming a sweeping claim the text cannot support.

You do not have to make the claim bigger. Make its significance clearer.
