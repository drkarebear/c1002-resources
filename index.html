Make Literary Analysis Easier

Literary analysis is not one giant skill. It is a set of smaller moves you can practice. Work on one move at a time, or start at the beginning and build from there.

Start from the beginning Choose what you want to practice
These are practice spaces, not tests. You can try again, read the feedback, and work at your own pace. Your answers are not recorded or submitted.

Choose a Move

If you know where you are getting stuck, go directly to that move. If you are not sure, begin with Notice.

1

Notice

See what the text is doing.

  • Tell summary from analysis
  • Notice details and patterns
  • Ask useful questions
  • Zoom in on language
Practice Notice
2

Interpret

Ask what the pattern might mean.

  • Move from observation to interpretation
  • Test whether a reading fits the evidence
  • Build thematic ideas
  • Analyze perspective
Practice Interpret
3

Support

Use evidence and explain it.

  • Connect claims to evidence
  • Integrate quotations
  • Explain how evidence supports an idea
Practice Support
4

Develop

Build and complicate an argument.

  • Strengthen a thesis
  • Revise when evidence complicates a claim
  • Repair weak analysis
Practice Develop

How to Use the Labs

  • Choose the move that matches where you feel stuck.
  • Read the feedback, even when your first answer is correct.
  • Try again when something does not work.
  • You do not need to complete every activity unless your instructor asks you to.
  • If your instructor requires proof, use the completion screen at the end of the activity.
