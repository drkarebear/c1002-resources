Literary Analysis Lab

Notice → Wonder

You do not have to know what a literary detail means the moment you notice it. A strong reader often pauses between seeing a pattern and making an interpretation. That pause is where useful questions begin.

What This Helps You Do

This activity helps you turn a textual observation into a question that can lead toward analysis.

NOTICE Identify something worth paying attention to: a repetition, contrast, image, word choice, action, or pattern.
→
WONDER Ask why that detail might matter, what it might suggest, or what larger idea it could connect to.
You are not trying to find the “right answer” yet. Your job is to ask a question that opens up interpretation instead of just requesting more plot information.

Round 1: See the Move

Read this short passage:

NOTICE

Which question best turns this observation into a useful wonder?

You practiced the move from noticing to wondering.

When you notice a detail, you do not need to rush to a thesis. Try asking:

  • Why might the writer keep drawing attention to this?
  • What changes because of this detail?
  • What larger idea could this pattern connect to?
  • What tension or contradiction does this create?

Those questions create a bridge from observation to interpretation.

Try It With Your Text

Choose one detail from the story, poem, or play you are reading now. In your notes or draft, write one question that begins with Why might…, What changes because…, or What larger idea could….

This low-stakes practice activity does not record or submit student responses.
