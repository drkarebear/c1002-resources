Theme or Topic?

Words like family, freedom, identity, and power are important subjects, but they are not themes by themselves. A theme is an idea the text develops about one of those subjects.

What This Helps You Do

This lab helps you move from a broad subject to a more specific thematic interpretation.

TOPIC A broad subject such as family, freedom, childhood, fear, or identity.
THEMATIC IDEA A statement about what the text may suggest about that subject.
THEMATIC INTERPRETATION A specific, defensible claim connected to patterns and evidence in the text.
Theme is not a one-word answer. Ask: “What does this text seem to suggest about this topic?”

You practiced moving from topic to theme.

When you think you have found a theme, test it:

  • Is it more than one word?
  • Does it say something about the topic?
  • Can I connect it to a pattern in the text?
  • Is it specific enough to argue but cautious enough to defend?

“Family” is a topic. “Belonging in a family can depend on unspoken expectations” is a thematic idea. The difference is the thinking.
