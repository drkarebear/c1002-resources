Literary Analysis Lab

Too Obvious, Too Wild, or Just Right?

Literary analysis asks you to go beyond what the text obviously says without traveling so far that the evidence can no longer support you. This lab helps you find that middle ground.

What This Helps You Do

As you read, interpretations often fall into one of three zones:

TOO OBVIOUSMostly restates the text or names a detail without explaining its significance.
TOO WILDMakes a claim that goes far beyond what the evidence can reasonably support.
JUST RIGHTMakes an interpretive claim that grows from the evidence and can be defended.
“Just right” does not mean “the one correct answer.” More than one interpretation can be defensible if the reader can show how the text supports it.

When you are unsure about a claim, ask:

  • Am I just repeating what the text already says?
  • What specific evidence gives me a reason to make this claim?
  • Am I adding assumptions the text never gives me?
  • Could I explain the connection between the evidence and my interpretation?

A strong interpretation reaches beyond the obvious, but it keeps one hand on the text.
