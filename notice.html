Move 1 of 4

1. Notice

See what the text is doing. Use these activities to get unstuck in a difficult passage, separate summary from analysis, notice details, ask useful questions, and begin seeing patterns in a text.

Choose one. Pick the activity that would help you most right now, unless your instructor assigned a specific one.

Notice Activities

Notice activity 1

Lost in the Text?

Find the exact place a difficult passage becomes confusing, repair enough meaning to continue, and return to the writer’s words.

If this sounds like you:
“I read the words, but I still don’t understand what this passage is saying.”

Open “Lost in the Text?”
Notice activity 2

What Kind of Thinking Is This?

Tell the difference between summary, observation, and analysis.

If this sounds like you:
“I understand what happened, but I don’t know what counts as analysis.”

Open “What Kind of Thinking Is This?”
Notice activity 3

Notice → Wonder

Turn something you notice into a question that can lead toward interpretation.

If this sounds like you:
“I noticed something, but I don’t know what question to ask about it.”

Open “Notice → Wonder”
Notice activity 4

Pattern Finder

Decide which details belong together and identify the pattern they create.

If this sounds like you:
“I have several details, but I don’t know how they connect.”

Open “Pattern Finder”
Notice activity 5

Zoom In

Focus closely on a word, image, contrast, or detail and ask what it does.

If this sounds like you:
“I found a quotation, but I don’t know what part of it to analyze.”

Open “Zoom In”
Notice activity 6

Writer’s Block: Go Back to the Text

If this sounds like you:
“I know I need to write more, but I don’t know what else to say.”

Return to the text, notice more, and create new analytical material before trying to make the paragraph longer.

Open “Writer’s Block”
