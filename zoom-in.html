Zoom In

Strong analysis often comes from looking closely at a small part of the evidence. Instead of explaining an entire quotation, you can focus on the word, image, contrast, or detail that does the most interpretive work.

Before You Practice: Slow Down Around the Smallest Useful Detail

Close reading does not require you to explain every word in a quotation. Often the strongest analysis begins when you identify one word or phrase that carries unusual weight and ask why that choice matters.

1. Find the DetailWhich word, image, contrast, action, or choice stands out?
2. Ask What It DoesWhat feeling, idea, tension, or relationship does it create?
3. Connect It BackHow does that detail support the larger interpretation?
Do not choose a word just because it sounds interesting. Choose the detail that gives you something useful to explain in relation to your interpretation.

You practiced zooming in on evidence.

When you use a quotation, try not to stop at: “This shows…” followed by a paraphrase.

Instead, ask:

  • Which word or image carries the most meaning?
  • What does that choice suggest?
  • What tension or contrast does it create?
  • How does it support my interpretation?

Close reading means making a larger idea from careful attention to a smaller detail.
