Literary Analysis Lab

Zoom In

Strong analysis often comes from looking closely at a small part of the evidence. Instead of explaining an entire quotation, you can focus on the word, image, contrast, or detail that does the most interpretive work.

What This Helps You Do

This lab helps you identify which part of a quotation is worth analyzing and explain why that detail matters to an interpretation.

1. Find the Detail Which word, image, contrast, or choice stands out?
2. Ask What It Does What feeling, idea, tension, or relationship does it create?
3. Connect It Back How does that detail support the larger interpretation?
You do not have to analyze every word in a quotation. Sometimes one carefully chosen word can give you more to say than an entire sentence.

You practiced zooming in on evidence.

When you use a quotation, try not to stop at: “This shows…” followed by a paraphrase.

Instead, ask:

  • Which word or image carries the most meaning?
  • What does that choice suggest?
  • What tension or contrast does it create?
  • How does it support my interpretation?

Close reading means making a larger idea from careful attention to a smaller detail.

This low-stakes practice activity does not record or submit student responses.
