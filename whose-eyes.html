Whose Eyes Are We Seeing Through?

Stories do not simply show us what happened. They shape what we notice by choosing whose perspective we follow, what that perspective values, and what it may misunderstand or leave out.

What This Helps You Do

This lab helps you analyze how perspective changes the meaning of the same event.

1. Notice the LensWho is seeing, thinking, or describing?
2. Notice the ValuesWhat does that perspective call important, strange, dangerous, or normal?
3. Interpret the EffectHow does that perspective shape what the reader understands?
Perspective is more than first person versus third person. Even a third-person narrator may keep us very close to one character’s understanding of the world.

You practiced analyzing perspective.

When you read, try asking:

  • Whose experience is closest to the reader?
  • What does this perspective notice that someone else might ignore?
  • What does the perspective value or fear?
  • What might this perspective misunderstand?
  • How would the scene change if someone else told it?

Perspective shapes meaning because it shapes what counts as important in the first place.
