3. Support

Claim, Evidence, Explain & Connect

A strong analytical paragraph has a job inside the larger essay. It makes a smaller claim, supports that claim with evidence, explains what the evidence means, and helps the reader see how that thinking contributes to the larger argument.

Keep in mind: These are paragraph jobs, not a four-sentence formula. You may use more than one piece of evidence, spend several sentences explaining a detail, or weave the connection into your analysis.

Before You Practice: The Jobs Inside an Analytical Paragraph

The thesis gives the whole essay its argument. Each body paragraph then takes on one smaller part of that argument. The paragraph's claim often appears in or near the topic sentence.

1. Four Jobs to Look For

CLAIMWhat is this paragraph trying to show? This is often the topic sentence or paragraph-level claim.
EVIDENCEWhat specific part of the text gives the writer a reason to make that claim?
EXPLAINWhat should the reader notice in the evidence? What does the detail suggest, reveal, complicate, or help us understand?
CONNECTWhy does this paragraph's analysis matter to the essay's larger thesis or interpretive problem?

2. Thesis and Topic Sentence Do Different Jobs

THESIS = whole essay
The story suggests that fear of social judgment can make conformity stronger than individual perception.
↓
PARAGRAPH CLAIM / TOPIC SENTENCE = one part of that argument
The adults' exaggerated praise shows how fear of embarrassment turns private doubt into public conformity.
↓
EVIDENCE + EXPLANATION
Now the paragraph has to show the reader why that smaller claim makes sense.
↓
CONNECT
The paragraph's smaller claim becomes one piece of the case for the thesis.

Useful default: A thesis usually falls near the end of the introduction. A topic sentence usually falls at or near the beginning of a body paragraph. These are reader-friendly conventions, not rigid laws.

3. Explanation Is Where the Analysis Usually Happens

A paragraph can have a strong claim and a perfectly relevant quotation and still feel unfinished if the writer never explains the evidence.

Restatement: This quote shows that the doorway is enormous.

Analysis: The word “enormous” exaggerates the doorway from the child's perspective, making the adult-controlled space feel overwhelming.

The second sentence gives the reader an inference. It tells us what the language does, not merely what it says.

4. Connection Does Not Mean “Repeat the Thesis”

A connection steps back and answers a larger question: What has this paragraph helped us understand?

It might show how the evidence supports the thesis, complicates the thesis, develops a larger theme, or changes what the reader understands about the text.

You do not need to end every paragraph with “This proves my thesis.” A good connection can be woven naturally into the final part of the analysis.

5. Watch the Four Jobs Work Together

Thesis

The story suggests that fear of social judgment can make conformity stronger than individual perception.

Claim / Topic Sentence

The adults' exaggerated praise shows how fear of embarrassment turns private doubt into public conformity.

Evidence

After adult after adult praises clothing they cannot see, the child finally says, “But he has nothing on.”

Explain

The child's blunt sentence contrasts with the adults' elaborate praise, making their agreement look more like a performance than genuine belief.

Connect

Once one person says what everyone can see, the performance becomes harder to maintain, supporting the larger idea that conformity depends partly on people believing no one else will challenge the group.

Notice: This does not have to be exactly four sentences. A real paragraph may move Evidence → Explain → Evidence → Explain before making the larger connection.

Try It With Your Paragraph

CLAIM → EVIDENCE → EXPLAIN → CONNECT

If one is missing, do not automatically add another quotation. Ask what intellectual job the paragraph still needs to do.
